\section{Approach}
The implementation of trace inference construction of Parent and Zucker~\cite{parent1989}
and the relaxation framework of Hummel and Zucker~\cite{hummel1983} are used on three images.
The three stages of curve inference were applied as follows.
The original image was grey-scaled and a convolution was applied.
The kernel was generated via a gaussian.
The rotation matrix was used to rotate the kernel and generate 16 convolved image yielding the orientation for each pixel.
Given such values for tangents, the properties of cocirculatrigy and curvature consistency were used to measure their support.
Radial relaxtation was further used to update them.

The examples used are spaghetti, fingerprint, and
hair. Spaghetti gives a relatively clear test of curve crossings.
Fingerprint has changing orientation while the hair image combines strands of different widths with highlights, shadows, and dense parallel structure.

\section{Part I: initial tangent estimates}
\subsection{Measurement model and normalization}
For each $\theta_n$, sampling coordinates are rotated as
$(x',y')=(x\cos\theta_n+y\sin\theta_n,-x\sin\theta_n+y\cos\theta_n)$.
The resulting kernel is normalized by its discrete sum to help control the intensity scaling introduced by the filter.
Convolution was applied with this kernel using symmetric image padding.
This helps remove artificial black borders and keeps the original image consistent.

If $f_i(n)$ denotes the response for orientation $n$, initialization is
\begin{equation}
p_i^{(0)}(n)=\frac{\exp(f_i(n)/\tau)}{\sum_{m=0}^{15}\exp(f_i(m)/\tau)}.
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
Softmax is evaluated with a numerically stable library implementation and the temperature is used as another tunable parameter.

\subsection{Orientation in image coordinates}
The reference kernel is vertical. Its tangent in continuous sampling coordinates
is proportional to $(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$. Horizontal and vertical sample
spacings are $\Delta x=4/30$ and $\Delta y=5/30$, so its image-pixel angle is
\begin{equation}
\phi_n=\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(\frac{\cos\theta_n}{\Delta y},
                              \frac{-\sin\theta_n}{\Delta x}\right)\bmod\pi.
\label{eq:angles}
\end{equation}
Rows increase downward, matching the displayed image coordinates. Using these
angles for both vectors and support keeps the geometry consistent with the
sampled kernels. A consequence is that the 16 pixel-space angles are not uniformly
spaced, although the specified kernel rotations are uniform.

\subsection{Initial results}
Increasing the display threshold from $p>0.20$ (Figure~\ref{fig:initial}) to
$p>0.50$ (Figure~\ref{fig:initial-p05}) reduces the displayed vectors from
8,685 to 2,794 for spaghetti, 14,319 to 790 for fingerprint, and 17,979 to
2,689 for hair, removing many competing directions.
Spaghetti retains clear tangent tracks along most strands, while fingerprint
and hair become sparser, with gaps where no orientation exceeds the higher threshold.
Since the initial probabilities sum to one at each pixel, $p>0.50$ permits at
most one displayed orientation per sampled pixel, although a high confidence
alone does not establish that its direction is correct. Thus, the latter value is used.

Spaghetti already has recognizable tangents, but crossings and endpoints
produce fans of conflicting directions. The negative central lobe also makes
boundaries and darker portions of the bright strands influential, rather than
consistently locating each strand's centerline. Fingerprint and hair have much
denser conflicting estimates. In the fingerprint crop, ridge-aligned vertical
vectors coexist with transverse vectors. In hair, many vectors follow the
prevailing diagonal direction, but other directions appear across the strands.

\section{Part II: cocircularity and curvature consistency}
\subsection{Cocircularity compatibility}
Following Parent and Zucker~\cite{parent1989}, two unit tangents $\lambda$ and
$\lambda'$ are cocircular when a circle is tangent to both at their respective
positions. Collinear tangents are the limiting case of infinite radius.
Their orientations are denoted $\theta_\lambda$ and $\theta_{\lambda'}$;
these are the image-coordinate angles $\phi_\lambda$ and $\phi_{\lambda'}$
from Part I, rather than the kernel rotation angles.

For pixels $(x_i,y_i)$ and $(x_j,y_j)$, define
\begin{equation}
\Delta x=x_j-x_i,\qquad \Delta y=y_j-y_i,\qquad
d_{ij}=\sqrt{\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2},\qquad
\theta_{ij}=\arctan(\Delta y/\Delta x).
\end{equation}
Here $\theta_{ij}$ is the orientation of the line joining the pixels, as in
the paper's Equation (5.1). The code evaluates it using
$\operatorname{atan2}(\Delta y,\Delta x)\bmod\pi$ to handle vertical lines.
The paper's signed interior-angle function is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma(\beta,\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
\gamma-\beta, & |\gamma-\beta|\leq\pi/2,\\
\gamma-\beta-\pi, & \pi/2<\gamma-\beta\leq\pi,\\
\gamma-\beta+\pi, & -\pi\leq\gamma-\beta<-\pi/2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Exact cocircularity requires equal interior angles,
$\Gamma(\theta_\lambda,\theta_{ij})=
\Gamma(\theta_{ij},\theta_{\lambda'})$.
For quantized positions and orientations, the implementation uses the paper's
discrete condition, Equation (5.5):
\begin{equation}
\left|\Gamma(\theta_\lambda,\theta_{ij})-
\Gamma(\theta_{ij},\theta_{\lambda'})\right|
<\epsilon+2\alpha,\qquad
\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{16},\quad \alpha=\arcsin(1/d_{ij}).
\label{eq:cocircularity}
\end{equation}
The term $\epsilon$ allows for orientation discretization, while $2\alpha$
allows for uncertainty in the joining line when each tangent position varies
within a radius-$1/2$ pixel circle. Integer offsets exclude $i=j$, so
$d_{ij}\geq1$.

\textbf{Assumption: simplified cocircularity coefficient.}
The implementation replaces the gradual falloff in the paper's Figure 9 and
Equation (5.6) with a step function:
\begin{equation}
c_{ij}(\lambda,\lambda')=
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if Equation~\eqref{eq:cocircularity} holds},\\
c_{\min}=0.1, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus, all pairs outside the accepted angular tolerance receive the same weak
compatibility, regardless of how far they depart from cocircularity.
The sloping transition regions shown in the paper's figure are not implemented.

\subsection{Curvature classes and support}
For a candidate tangent $\phi$ at $i$, the signed curvature of the circle through
$j$ tangent at $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{ij}(\phi)=
\frac{2(\cos\phi\,\Delta y-\sin\phi\,\Delta x)}{d_{ij}^2}.
\label{eq:curvature}
\end{equation}
The seven bin edges are
\begin{equation}
(-0.20,-0.16,-0.12,-0.08,0.08,0.12,0.16,0.20).
\end{equation}
The central bin represents approximately straight structure. Its limit is
$\min(2/r^2,\kappa_{\max}/2)=0.08$, based on a one-pixel transverse displacement
at the neighborhood boundary. The remaining bins divide the negative and
positive ranges evenly. The cutoff $|\kappa|\leq0.2$ excludes circular radii below
5 pixels. Intervals are left-closed and right-open, except that the final endpoint
is included. Out-of-range curvature receives class $-1$ and contributes no
class-supported evidence.

Let $b_{ij}(\lambda)$ be the bin of $\kappa_{ij}(\phi_\lambda)$ and let
$k_j(\lambda')$ be a previously estimated winning class at $j$. In the reversed
chord direction, $b_{ji}(\lambda')$ bins $-\kappa_{ij}(\phi_{\lambda'})$.
The implemented class-specific support is
\begin{align}
S_{ik}(\lambda)&=\sum_{j\in N_r(i)}\sum_{\lambda'}
 c_{ij}(\lambda,\lambda')\,
 \mathbf1\{b_{ij}(\lambda)=k\}\,
 \mathbf1\{b_{ji}(\lambda')=k_j(\lambda')\geq0\}\,p_j(\lambda'),\\
s_i(\lambda)&=\max_k S_{ik}(\lambda),\qquad
k_i(\lambda)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_k S_{ik}(\lambda).
\label{eq:support}
\end{align}
This is a discrete mutual-membership version of the paper's curvature-class and
curvature-consistency factors. Curvature signs are interpreted relative to the
chosen tangent and chord directions, rather than by requiring equal signed
class indices at opposite endpoints.

Initially no winning classes are available. An ungated pass omits the second
indicator to estimate them; a second pass then computes class-consistent support.
During relaxation, each pass uses classes from the preceding pass and selects
new winners. A hypothesis with zero support is marked class $-1$.

\subsection{Neighborhood and computation}
The neighborhood contains the 80 nonzero integer offsets inside a radius-5 disk.
This limits support to nearby structure and avoids mixing distant curves. The
maximum over classes favors one local curvature interpretation instead of
summing incompatible interpretations. Lateral maxima selection is omitted, as
specified in the assignment. This leaves multiple responses across a thick
strand, so the output is a tangent field rather than a thinned centerline.

Compatibility matrices and curvature-bin memberships are precomputed for each
offset. Neighbor orientations are accumulated with matrix operations, and row
tiles bound intermediate memory use. Support neighborhoods are clipped at image
boundaries, with neither wraparound nor self-support. Boundary pixels therefore
have fewer possible supporting neighbors than interior pixels.

For these overlays only, raw support is divided by its global maximum. The
threshold is the 90th percentile of normalized per-pixel support maxima, rounded
to two decimals. A displayed tangent must also have initial confidence above
0.20. This produces an image-specific display criterion, not an assertion that
exactly 10\% of candidates survive. Raw support is retained for relaxation.

Support retains 6,231 of 8,685 sampled spaghetti candidates, or 71.7\%. Most
strand-aligned tracks remain while many transverse vectors at intersections
are removed. In fingerprint, only 2,213 of 14,319 candidates remain, or 15.5\%.
The central crop loses much of its transverse clutter, but also loses portions
of visually plausible ridge tangents. In hair, 3,243 of 17,979 remain, or 18.0\%,
with a clearer preference for the dominant strand direction.

These reductions reflect both geometric support and a deliberately selective
display threshold. They do not establish an accuracy rate. Strong support may
also occur along the finger boundary, shadows, or adjacent parallel strands,
which are locally coherent without necessarily being the intended curves.

\section{Part III: radial relaxation labeling}
\subsection{Update rule and reference scale}
The support score is
\begin{equation}
A(p)=\sum_i\sum_\lambda p_i(\lambda)s_i(\lambda).
\end{equation}
Plots divide this sum by the number of pixels $N$, allowing images of different
sizes to be compared. Raw support is mapped to signed evidence as
\begin{equation}
q_i(\lambda)=\frac{s_i(\lambda)-s_{\min}}{s_{\max}-s_{\min}}.
\end{equation}
The reference $s_{\max}=2r=10$ is the support of a unit-confidence horizontal
line through the neighborhood. Using a minimum line confidence of 0.6 gives
$s_{\min}=0.6s_{\max}/2=3$, following the endpoint argument in Eq.~6.15 of
Parent and Zucker~\cite{parent1989}. This is a fixed reference, not a measured
upper bound: support can exceed it and $q$ is not clipped to $[-1,1]$.

Each orientation has its own tangent versus no-line pair $[p,1-p]$, with evidence
$[q,-q]$. Subtracting its minimum gives nonnegative evidence
$[2\max(q,0),2\max(-q,0)]$. Adding a step of this evidence and normalizing the
pair yields the binary radial rule from Appendix A of the trace inference paper:
\begin{equation}
p^{(t+1)}=\frac{p^{(t)}+2\eta\max(q,0)}{1+2\eta|q|}.
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
Positive $q$ strengthens a tangent, negative $q$ weakens it, and $q=0$ preserves
it. Valid input confidences remain in $[0,1]$. Orientations are not renormalized
against one another after initialization, so multiple directions can survive a
crossing and their confidences can sum to more than one.

After each update, support is recomputed using the preceding curvature classes.
The run stops after five updates or when the maximum absolute confidence change
is at most $10^{-4}$. All three images reached the five-update limit.

\section{Results after five updates}
\subsection{Support histories}
Spaghetti's support per pixel falls from 1.049 to 0.820 on the first update, then
rises to 2.270 by update five. Fingerprint falls slightly from 1.391 to 1.363,
then reaches 5.192. Hair increases from 1.610 to 7.005. The final scores are
approximately 2.16, 3.73, and 4.35 times their initial values, respectively.

The first decreases show why a monotonic-improvement claim is inappropriate
here. The maximum over curvature classes and the changing winning classes make
the effective compatibility depend on the current state. In addition, independent
binary labels allow total tangent confidence to grow, so a larger $A(p)$ does
not by itself establish more accurate curve localization. The maximum changes
on update five are still 0.510, 0.505, and 0.433. These values indicate substantial
remaining movement, rather than convergence to a fixed labeling.

Spaghetti has the clearest visual improvement. Tangents follow the strands more
consistently and many fans of transverse vectors disappear. Directions associated
with intersecting strands remain visible near the central crossing. However,
multiple nearly parallel tracks persist across strand widths; there is no
centerline selection or explicit reconstruction of which strand passes over
another.

Fingerprint retains a more organized tangent field along the upper and right-hand
ridge patterns, but the central crop remains incomplete. Several plausible
vertical ridge tangents survive, while gaps and isolated horizontal fragments
remain. Hair increasingly favors the main diagonal direction, yet detections
spread across wide bands and some dark gaps between strands. Local consistency
improves directional organization without guaranteeing that every surviving
vector lies on a distinct physical strand.

\section{Crossings, assumptions, and limitations}
\subsection{Controlled crossing}
\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering\asset{0.88}{synthetic_crossing.png}
\caption{A controlled crossing and an isolated tangent. The separate test configuration uses radius 3, one curvature class with edges $[-0.05,0.05]$, $c_{\min}=0$, support limits 1 and 6, and five updates.}
\label{fig:crossing}
\end{figure}
The synthetic example begins with confidence 0.7 on each of two perpendicular
lines and 0.8 on a separate isolated tangent. After five updates, both crossing
confidences are approximately 0.998, while the isolated confidence is 0.149.
This verifies that the update can preserve two supported directions at the same
pixel while suppressing an unsupported one. The example starts from assigned
confidences and does not test the convolution stage or identify physical strands
at an occlusion.

A bifurcation requires more than preserving two orientations: one incoming trace
must connect plausibly to its outgoing branches. The fingerprint examples do not
provide annotated bifurcations or a trace-linking stage, so successful branch
recovery is not established. The real spaghetti crossing and the controlled test
support a narrower conclusion about retaining multiple tangent hypotheses.

\subsection{Consequences of the simplifying assumptions}
The finite radius keeps support local, but also limits gap bridging and penalizes
endpoints or image boundaries with fewer neighbors. Seven curvature classes
provide a coarse local shape model; their broad central bin tolerates nearly
straight structure, while the maximum-curvature cutoff can reject tight bends.
The fixed cocircularity tolerance also approximates quantization even though
pixel-space angles are nonuniform after the sampling-scale correction.

A single filter scale cannot simultaneously match thin ridges and wide strands.
The selected negative-center template can respond to dark grooves or strand
boundaries rather than bright centerlines. Strong directional structure can
therefore survive in the wrong spatial location. Omitting lateral maxima
selection leaves duplicate tracks and broad tangent bands. These effects are
especially visible in hair and spaghetti.

The support reference scale is based on a horizontal ideal line, without adjusting
for orientation-dependent lattice counts, local contrast, or truncated
neighborhoods. Independent binary labels preserve crossings, but permit several
nearby or incorrect directions to accumulate confidence together. Finally, five
updates are enough to inspect the mechanism, not enough to establish convergence.

The strongest result is suppression of conflicting directions followed by more
coherent tangent tracks. The weakest result is precise localization in dense,
low-contrast patterns. There are no annotated traces or orientation labels for
these inputs, so precision, recall, and angular error are not reported. The
vector counts and support histories are internal measurements, accompanied by
visual inspection rather than treated as ground-truth accuracy.

\section{Conclusion}
Cocircularity and curvature consistency remove many ambiguous initial tangents,
and radial relaxation strengthens locally compatible directions. Spaghetti shows
clearer strand-aligned tracks after five updates; fingerprint and hair show
stronger directional organization with remaining gaps, false fragments, and broad
responses. The controlled crossing confirms that independent tangent labels can
retain two supported directions. Higher support alone does not prove accurate
curve recovery, and none of the reported real-image runs has converged.
